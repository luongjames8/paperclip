# Step 4: Targeted Research

**Version:** 1.0
**Last Updated:** 2026-01-25

## Purpose

Execute research queries generated by narrative gap analysis to find missing narrative elements.

This step answers: "What specific facts can fill our GROUND, IMPLICATE, and LAND gaps?"

Unlike full research (which explores broadly), targeted research has a specific mission: find facts that serve a named narrative function. Each query comes with a purpose (GROUND/IMPLICATE/LAND) and a gap description. Find material that fills exactly that gap.

## Process

### Step 1: Prioritize Queries

Order queries by priority:
1. **critical** - Execute first, invest most effort
2. **important** - Execute after critical queries
3. **nice_to_have** - Execute only if time/context permits

If there are more than 5 queries, focus on critical and important only.

### Step 2: Execute Each Query

For each research query:

```
1. Locate pre-fetched search results for this query (from pre_fetched_data above)
   - Review top 5-10 results
   - Prioritize recent sources (within 2 years for LAND queries)
   - Prioritize primary sources (testimony, documents, interviews)

2. For each promising URL in the pre-fetched data:
   a. Read the pre-fetched full page content
   b. Search for facts that fill the specific gap
   c. Extract concrete details: names, numbers, dates, places
   d. Note the emotional register (grief, irony, outrage, etc.)

3. For GROUND queries (victim/character stories):
   - Locate pre-fetched YouTube results for testimony, interviews, documentaries
   - Read pre-fetched video transcripts for relevant segments
   - Look for first-person accounts, named individuals

4. Write findings to narrative_material BEFORE moving to next query
```

### Step 3: Function-Specific Extraction

#### For GROUND Items

Looking for: People, places, specific moments that make abstract harm concrete.

| Extract | Example |
|---------|---------|
| Named victim | "Sarah Johnson lost her son Tyler, 23, to opioid overdose in 2019" |
| Specific moment | "At the congressional hearing, she held up his graduation photo" |
| Named villain decision | "Martin Elling personally approved the 'turbocharge sales' plan" |
| Place with weight | "The McKinsey Boston office where the strategy was developed" |

GROUND items should answer: "Who suffered? Who decided? What moment captures it?"

#### For IMPLICATE Items

Looking for: Headlines, coverage, narratives that viewer would have seen and believed.

| Extract | Example |
|---------|---------|
| Original headline | "McKinsey to Pay $573 Million to Settle Opioid Claims - NYT, Feb 2021" |
| Public narrative | "Settlement was framed as 'accountability' and 'turning the page'" |
| What viewer believed | "Most readers assumed the settlement meant justice was served" |
| The gap revealed | "No criminal charges, no admission of wrongdoing, charges dropped in 2024" |

IMPLICATE items should answer: "What did the viewer believe? How were they misled?"

#### For LAND Items

Looking for: Current status, ongoing harm, unresolved tension, "this isn't over."

| Extract | Example |
|---------|---------|
| Where are they now | "Kevin Sneader is now Chairman of Asia-Pacific for McKinsey" |
| Ongoing harm | "Opioid deaths reached 110,000 in 2024, still rising" |
| Unresolved tension | "The consultants who designed the strategy have never been named" |
| Future implications | "Similar consulting tactics now used for [other harmful product]" |

LAND items should answer: "Why does this matter NOW? What isn't over?"

### Step 4: Atomize Findings

Each finding becomes a separate item. Do not combine multiple facts into one item.

| Bad (combined) | Good (atomized) |
|----------------|-----------------|
| "Sarah Johnson lost her son Tyler to opioids and testified before Congress holding his photo" | Item 1: "Sarah Johnson lost her son Tyler, 23, to opioid overdose in 2019" <br> Item 2: "At congressional testimony, Johnson held Tyler's graduation photo while describing his death" |

### Step 5: Tag with Specifics

Every item must have specifics extracted:

```yaml
specifics:
  names: ["Sarah Johnson", "Tyler Johnson"]
  numbers: ["23 years old"]
  dates: ["2019", "congressional hearing 2020"]
  places: ["Ohio"]
```

Items without specifics are too abstract. Either dig deeper or note the item as weak.

### Step 6: Document Query Results

For each query executed:

```yaml
query_results:
  - query: "opioid overdose victim family McKinsey testimony"
    purpose: "GROUND"
    priority: "critical"
    status: "found"
    items_found: 3
    notes: "Found congressional testimony with named victims"
```

Status values:
- **found**: Gap is filled with 2+ quality items
- **partial**: Found some material but gap not fully filled
- **not_found**: No relevant material found

### Step 7: Assess Gaps Remaining

After all queries:

```yaml
functions_filled:
  GROUND: true   # If at least 2 items with specifics
  IMPLICATE: true
  LAND: false    # If fewer than 2 items

gaps_remaining:
  - "LAND: Could not find current status of Martin Elling"
```

---

## Hard Constraints

- Execute queries in priority order (critical first)
- Each item gets exactly ONE narrative function tag
- Every item must have at least one specific (name, number, date, or place)
- No invented facts - only extract what sources contain
- Source every item with URL and date
- Atomize - one fact per item
- Fetch full content, not just headlines
- For LAND queries, prioritize recent sources (2024-2026)

---

## Source Credibility Assessment

| Credibility | Criteria |
|-------------|----------|
| **HIGH** | Congressional testimony, court documents, first-person accounts, official reports |
| **MEDIUM** | Major publication, named sources, verifiable claims |
| **LOW** | Aggregator, anonymous sources, opinion pieces |

For narrative material, prioritize HIGH credibility sources. GROUND items especially need credible firsthand accounts.

---

## Depth Requirements

### Per Query

- Search results: Review top 5-10 URLs
- Fetch: Read full content of 3-5 most promising sources
- Extract: 2-5 items per successful query

### Per Item

- Must have specifics (names, numbers, dates, places)
- Must have clear emotional register
- Must clearly fill the stated gap

### When Queries Return Thin Results

If a query returns poor results:

1. Reformulate query with different terms
2. Try YouTube search for video content (interviews, testimony)
3. If still thin, note as `status: partial` or `status: not_found`
4. Do NOT invent material to fill gaps
